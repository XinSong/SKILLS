# Chinese technical translation style

Read this file before drafting or editing a Chinese translation.

## Priority order

1. Preserve the source claim, scope, certainty, and attribution.
2. Preserve every meaningful source unit without adding target-only claims.
3. Use one Chinese rendering for each technical term throughout the document.
4. Write natural, concise Chinese inside each Markdown block.
5. Preserve the source tone. Do not make informal prose academic or make
   qualified prose categorical.

## Expression

- Translate meaning rather than copying English word order.
- Resolve pronouns only when the source referent is clear. Preserve ambiguity
  when it is not.
- Prefer established Chinese technical terms. On first occurrence, retain the
  English term in parentheses when it aids recognition or the glossary requires
  it.
- Keep product names, APIs, commands, identifiers, filenames, code, formulas,
  and URLs unchanged.
- Preserve modal force: distinguish `may`, `can`, `should`, and `must`; preserve
  hedges such as `likely`, `typically`, and `approximately`.
- Avoid filler such as “值得注意的是”, “众所周知”, “总而言之”, or an added
  conclusion unless it exists in the source.
- Avoid translationese such as unnecessary passive voice, repeated subjects,
  and chains of “的”. Restructure sentences within the same block when needed.

## Editing boundary

The Chinese editing pass may improve syntax, cohesion, and terminology only.
It may not delete repetition that exists in the source, resolve an argument,
add background knowledge, or turn the translation into a summary.
